# Mmentum Vision

## Core Thesis

Mmentum turns identity-based habits into a calm daily practice.

A habit can be as simple as a yes/no question or as rich as a scheduled plan. The application shows only what matters today, records meaningful evidence, and visualizes consistency without punishing imperfection.

The product follows a simple chain:

> Identity -> habits -> daily evidence -> momentum

We are not tracking tasks for their own sake. Each action is a vote for the person someone wants to become.

## A Rich Habit

A habit may contain:

- **Meaning:** The identity it supports
- **Cadence:** Daily, scheduled days, or a weekly frequency
- **Ways to make progress:** One action or several qualifying actions
- **Plan:** Optional instructions for what to do on a particular day
- **Definition of done:** What counts as meaningful evidence
- **History:** The actions actually taken
- **Momentum:** The accumulated pattern over time

These are capabilities, not required fields. A simple habit must remain simple.

## Different Habits, One Model

### Diet

- **Identity:** I am someone who eats intentionally.
- **Habit:** Stay within my calorie target.
- **Cadence:** Daily.
- **Today:** Did I follow my diet?
- **Evidence:** Yes, partially, or no.

### Physical

- **Identity:** I am someone who trains consistently and takes care of my body.
- **Habit:** Follow my movement plan.
- **Cadence:** Scheduled.
- **Today:** Walk for 20 minutes, Full Body A, Full Body B, or planned rest.
- **Evidence:** Whether I did what the plan called for.

### Career

- **Identity:** I am someone who invests in my craft and career.
- **Habit:** Make meaningful career progress.
- **Cadence:** Three or four times per week.
- **Ways to progress:** Side project, career research, resume work, or algorithm study.
- **Evidence:** Which meaningful action I took.

The physical habit needs a detailed plan. The career habit only needs a menu of qualifying actions. The diet habit may remain a single question. They should not be forced into the same configuration depth merely because they share the same underlying concept.

## Design Principles

### Identity Before Productivity

The product helps someone become a person, not merely clear a list.

### Habits Are Richer Than Checkboxes

A habit can contain meaning, cadence, guidance, alternatives, and progression.

### Today Stays Simple

Richness lives beneath the surface. Daily use shows what applies today and asks for the smallest honest response.

### Plans Are Optional

Add structure where it removes decision fatigue. Never require a plan where a simple definition of progress is enough.

### Evidence Can Take Different Forms

Some habits are binary. Some are scheduled. Some allow several actions to count.

### Momentum Replaces Streak Anxiety

Missing one opportunity slows momentum. It does not erase someone's history or reset their identity.

### Consistency Matters More Than Perfection

Three meaningful career sessions this week can represent success even if none happened on Tuesday.

### Reflection Should Not Become Administration

Recording progress must remain easier than doing the action itself.

### Depth Appears in Context

Like Linear, the habit looks concise in the everyday view and becomes rich only when opened or when the current day requires that depth.

### Design Should Encourage, Not Judge

The system should communicate continued possibility, especially after an imperfect day.

## Visualizing Momentum

The momentum visualization must embody this philosophy rather than merely copy GitHub's contribution graph. Each completed action adds energy. Consistent actions compound. Inactivity creates gradual drag, not catastrophic failure. Planned rest does not count against someone.

This is where the Newton's cradle metaphor becomes meaningful: each atomic action transfers energy into the identity someone is building. The calendar shows those individual inputs; momentum expresses what they have built together.
